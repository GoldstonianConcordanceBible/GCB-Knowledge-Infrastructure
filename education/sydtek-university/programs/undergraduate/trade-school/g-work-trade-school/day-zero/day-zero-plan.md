# Day Zero Plan

## G-Work x SydTek University

Day Zero is the foundational launch protocol for G-Work inside SydTek University.

It is not a public launch.

It is a controlled pilot designed to prove the trust loop before expanding into a full marketplace.

## Objective

Day Zero exists to make G-Work real through a controlled, high-trust environment.

Success means:

- First verified workers exist
- First customers are identified
- First real jobs are completed
- First ratings are captured
- First Digital Union status levels are assigned

## Core Principle

Fluency, not exposure.

G-Work does not begin by talking about skilled labor.

G-Work begins by organizing skilled labor, completing jobs, documenting outcomes, and building trust.

## Day Zero Definition

Day Zero is the moment the G-Work network becomes operational.

A worker is verified.

A customer requests work.

A job is completed.

A review is captured.

A standard is born.

## Success Targets

Within the first 7 to 14 days:

- 10 to 25 workers identified
- 5 to 10 customers seeded
- 10 to 20 jobs completed
- 100 percent of jobs documented
- First ratings collected
- First Verified and Certified workers identified

## Pilot Rule

Do not scale before trust is proven.

The first version should be manual, controlled, local, and relationship-driven.

## What Not To Do

Do not launch nationally.

Do not run mass ads.

Do not overbuild the app.

Do not introduce a token.

Do not issue NFT credentials yet.

Do not onboard unverified workers.

Do not sacrifice trust for speed.

## What Must Happen

The Day Zero pilot must prove three things:

1. Customers trust the system.
2. Workers want more work from the system.
3. The verification layer creates real value.
