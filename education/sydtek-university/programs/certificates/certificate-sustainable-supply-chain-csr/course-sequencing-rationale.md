# Course Sequencing Rationale
## Certificate in Sustainable Supply Chain Management & Corporate Social Responsibility

This document explains why the courses appear in their current order.

---

## Sequence

1. SSCM 101 — Foundations of Sustainable Supply Chains  
2. SSCM 201 — Corporate Social Responsibility, Ethics, and Stakeholder Governance  
3. SSCM 301 — Technology for Supply Chain Transparency and Resilience  
4. SSCM 401 — Capstone: Sustainable Supply Chain Strategy Lab  

---

## Why SSCM 101 Comes First

Students must first understand what a supply chain is before they can evaluate it morally, strategically, or technologically.

This course introduces:

- system structure
- flow logic
- stakeholder relationships
- fragility and disruption
- sustainability basics

Without this foundation, later courses risk becoming abstract or overly rhetorical.

---

## Why SSCM 201 Comes Second

Once students understand the structure of supply chains, they are ready to examine responsibility, labor, sourcing, stakeholder trust, and governance.

This order matters because students need an operational frame before they can meaningfully ask:

- who is harmed
- where responsibility lies
- what ethical sourcing means
- how governance failure spreads risk

SSCM 201 deepens the moral and organizational seriousness of the program.

---

## Why SSCM 301 Comes Third

Technology evaluation works best after students understand both system structure and governance concerns.

If taught too early, students may mistake tools for solutions.

Placing SSCM 301 third helps students judge technology in context.
They can ask:

- what problem is actually being solved
- what data is required
- what visibility means
- what governance remains necessary
- what limits the tool still has

This creates better discernment and reduces hype-driven thinking.

---

## Why SSCM 401 Comes Last

The capstone must come after the first three courses because it requires synthesis.

Students are expected to combine:

- operational understanding
- ethics and governance
- sustainability reasoning
- technology evaluation
- executive communication

The capstone is therefore not just another course.
It is the program’s integrative demonstration point.

---

## Design Principle

The overall sequence moves from:

**understanding**  
to  
**judging**  
to  
**evaluating tools**  
to  
**building strategy**

That progression is intentional and should generally be preserved.

---

## Adaptation Note

Programs may compress time or overlap delivery in special cases, but they should avoid letting students begin the capstone without substantial exposure to the first three courses.
