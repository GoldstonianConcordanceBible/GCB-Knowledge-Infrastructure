# Sample Grading Comments

This file provides example grading language for instructors across the certificate.

The goal is to improve consistency, clarity, and teaching value in faculty feedback.

---

## Strong Comment Examples

### Example 1
Strong systems thinking here.
You correctly show that the bottleneck is not the final shipment stage but the upstream packaging dependency.

### Example 2
Your memo is especially effective because it distinguishes public-relations language from governance reality.

### Example 3
This is a realistic recommendation.
You avoided the common mistake of assuming technology alone would solve a control problem.

### Example 4
Your strategy is well prioritized.
You identified what needs immediate action and what should wait for later implementation.

---

## Growth Comment Examples

### Example 1
Your analysis uses strong vocabulary, but several claims remain too abstract.
Name the exact operational changes you are recommending.

### Example 2
You identify the ethical concern clearly, but the memo needs more explanation of who is accountable and what control failed.

### Example 3
The technology section is promising, but it still reads as tool-first rather than problem-first.
Start with the operational need, then justify the tool.

### Example 4
Your strategy includes too many simultaneous changes.
Revise by ranking the top three interventions and explaining the sequence.

---

## Writing-Focused Comment Examples

### Example 1
Your ideas are solid, but the structure needs improvement.
Use clearer section headings and shorter paragraphs to help the reader follow your reasoning.

### Example 2
Several sentences sound persuasive but do not yet carry enough evidence.
Add a concrete example or explanation after each major claim.

### Example 3
This is thoughtful work, but the tone shifts between academic and conversational.
Revise for one consistent professional voice.

---

## Capstone Comment Examples

### Example 1
Your capstone succeeds because it integrates sustainability, governance, and technology without treating them as separate conversations.

### Example 2
The diagnosis is clear, but the KPI section needs stronger ownership logic.
Who will track each measure, and how often?

### Example 3
The final report is ambitious, but the implementation roadmap needs more realism around time, cost, and resistance.

### Example 4
You identified the right risks, but the prioritization logic needs to be more explicit.
Why is this issue first and not second?
