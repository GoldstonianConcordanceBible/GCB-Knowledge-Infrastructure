# Alignment Map
## Certificate in Sustainable Supply Chain Management & Corporate Social Responsibility

This document maps the internal logic of the certificate so it can be reviewed more easily for academic coherence, quality assurance, and future accreditation alignment.

---

## Purpose of This Document

The purpose of this map is to show that the certificate is not a loose collection of topics.

It is a sequenced program in which:

- learning outcomes build across courses
- assessments measure meaningful competencies
- portfolio artifacts provide visible evidence
- the capstone integrates the entire pathway

---

## Program Logic Overview

### Stage 1 — Foundations
**Course:** SSCM 101 — Foundations of Sustainable Supply Chains

Students learn the structure of supply chains, the meaning of sustainability in operations, and the relationship between risk, visibility, resilience, and responsibility.

### Stage 2 — Ethics and Governance
**Course:** SSCM 201 — Corporate Social Responsibility, Ethics, and Stakeholder Governance

Students move from structural understanding to ethical and governance analysis.
They learn how responsibility must be embedded in sourcing, labor, leadership, and public trust.

### Stage 3 — Technology and Visibility
**Course:** SSCM 301 — Technology for Supply Chain Transparency and Resilience

Students evaluate the digital systems now shaping supply chain decision-making, including AI, blockchain, IoT, analytics, and digital twins.

### Stage 4 — Integration and Application
**Course:** SSCM 401 — Capstone: Sustainable Supply Chain Strategy Lab

Students synthesize all previous learning into a practical strategy for a real or hypothetical organization.

---

## Program Learning Flow

### Students first learn to:
- define supply chain systems
- identify risk and fragility
- map stakeholders and flows

### Then they learn to:
- assess ethics and governance
- identify labor and sourcing concerns
- distinguish symbolic CSR from embedded responsibility

### Then they learn to:
- evaluate technology options
- judge transparency systems
- understand the risks and limits of digital tools

### Finally, they learn to:
- prioritize interventions
- build realistic strategies
- communicate recommendations at an executive level

---

## Internal Alignment Principles

This certificate is aligned around five internal principles:

### 1. Systems Thinking
Students must understand interconnected flows rather than isolated functions.

### 2. Ethical Reasoning
Students must interpret responsibility as operational, not merely rhetorical.

### 3. Applied Judgment
Students must make realistic recommendations rather than abstract claims.

### 4. Evidence Production
Students must produce visible artifacts that demonstrate competence.

### 5. Strategic Integration
Students must connect sustainability, governance, resilience, and technology in one frame.

---

## Signature Evidence Points

The certificate’s strongest evidence of alignment appears in the progression from:

- map
- memo
- evaluation brief
- final strategy

This means students are not repeatedly doing the same kind of work.
They are building toward more sophisticated forms of analysis.

The certificate is aligned as a staged program that begins with operational literacy, deepens into ethical and governance reasoning, expands into technology evaluation, and culminates in practical strategy design.
