# Moderation Guide
## Certificate in Sustainable Supply Chain Management & Corporate Social Responsibility

This guide supports more consistent grading and review across instructors, sections, and delivery formats.

---

## Purpose

Moderation is the process of checking whether assignments are being evaluated consistently and fairly.

This matters especially in a certificate built around writing, judgment, and portfolio artifacts rather than only objective tests.

---

## What Should Be Moderated

Program leadership should periodically review:

- assignment interpretation
- rubric use
- grading consistency
- feedback quality
- capstone expectations
- portfolio standards

---

## Recommended Moderation Points

### 1. Early Assignment Review
After the first major assignment in each course, instructors should compare a few sample submissions and discuss how the rubric is being applied.

### 2. Mid-Course Review
Faculty should review whether students are receiving consistent signals about quality, structure, and expectations.

### 3. Capstone Review
Capstone submissions should receive the strongest moderation because they represent program-level mastery.

### 4. Portfolio Review
Portfolio artifacts should be checked for consistency in revision standards and professionalism.

---

## Suggested Moderation Method

For each review cycle:

1. Select a small sample of student work across performance levels.
2. Compare rubric application.
3. Identify where instructors are aligned.
4. Identify where interpretations differ.
5. Clarify expectations in writing for future use.

---

## Common Moderation Questions

- Are instructors rewarding vocabulary more than understanding?
- Are realistic recommendations being valued more than inflated ambition?
- Are students being told clearly when work is too abstract?
- Are portfolio artifacts being held to a higher revision standard than drafts?
- Is the capstone being treated as an integrative strategy project rather than a loose essay?

---

## Evidence of Effective Moderation

Strong moderation should result in:

- clearer assignment expectations
- more consistent feedback
- stronger alignment between rubric and grading practice
- fewer mixed signals to students
- improved portfolio quality over time

---

## Recommended Outputs

After moderation, leadership should document:

- common strengths
- recurring student weaknesses
- rubric confusion points
- agreed interpretations
- recommended faculty adjustments

---

## Final Principle

Moderation should strengthen teaching, not burden it.

The goal is not rigid uniformity.
The goal is fair, clear, and academically credible evaluation.
