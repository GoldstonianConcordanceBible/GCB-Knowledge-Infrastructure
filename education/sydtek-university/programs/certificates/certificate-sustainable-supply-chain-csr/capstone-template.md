## 1. Executive Summary

Provide a concise overview of:

- the organization or sector
- the main supply chain problem
- the key risks
- the recommended intervention strategy
- the expected impact

Recommended length:
250 to 400 words

---

## 2. Organization and Context

Explain:

- what the organization does
- what product or service is being analyzed
- why the supply chain matters in this case
- what assumptions are being made if data is limited

Recommended length:
250 to 500 words

---

## 3. Current-State Supply Chain Diagnosis

Describe the current system.

Address:

- supplier structure
- logistics flow
- visibility level
- likely fragility points
- known or likely sustainability concerns
- ethical or governance concerns

Recommended length:
400 to 700 words

---

## 4. Risk Prioritization

Identify the most important risks.

Use categories such as:

- operational risk
- environmental risk
- labor or ethical risk
- governance risk
- reputational risk
- regulatory risk

For each major risk, explain:

- why it matters
- how severe it is
- how likely it is
- what it affects

Recommended length:
400 to 700 words

---

## 5. Sustainability and CSR Intervention Plan

Explain what should change in the operating model.

Possible categories include:

- sourcing practices
- supplier oversight
- labor protection
- packaging or waste reduction
- transparency measures
- accountability systems
- reporting improvements
- stakeholder communication

Recommended length:
500 to 800 words

---

## 6. Technology Recommendation Layer

Explain whether the organization should use tools such as:

- AI
- blockchain
- IoT
- analytics systems
- digital twins
- dashboards or reporting tools

For each recommendation, explain:

- what problem it solves
- why it fits
- what data it needs
- what risks remain
- what governance is required

Recommended length:
400 to 700 words

---

## 7. KPI and Measurement Framework

List the key indicators that should be tracked.

Possible KPI categories include:

- supplier compliance
- disruption frequency
- lead time stability
- emissions-related indicators
- audit findings
- visibility coverage
- incident response speed
- stakeholder trust signals

For each KPI, explain:

- what is being measured
- why it matters
- who should own the metric
- how often it should be reviewed

Recommended length:
300 to 500 words

---

## 8. Implementation Roadmap

Break the plan into phases.

### Example Structure
- Phase 1 — Immediate actions
- Phase 2 — Near-term redesign
- Phase 3 — Systems integration
- Phase 4 — Review and refinement

For each phase, explain:

- key actions
- responsible parties
- expected barriers
- measures of progress

Recommended length:
400 to 700 words

---

## 9. Tradeoffs and Constraints

Be honest about limitations.

Address:

- cost constraints
- time constraints
- change management difficulty
- data limitations
- supplier resistance
- leadership resistance
- technological uncertainty

Recommended length:
250 to 400 words

---

## 10. Final Reflection

Conclude with a brief reflection on:

- what matters most in this case
- why the chosen priorities are realistic
- how sustainability, responsibility, resilience, and technology must work together

Recommended length:
200 to 350 words

---

## 11. Appendices

Optional appendices may include:

- supply chain diagram
- risk matrix
- KPI table
- implementation timeline
- technology comparison matrix
- stakeholder map

---

## Submission Formats

Students may submit the capstone as:

- a formal written report
- a slide deck with supporting memo
- a GitHub-native strategy package

Faculty may specify the preferred format.
