# Model SSCM 201 Artifact
## Board Memo on Stakeholder Risk

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Student Analyst  
**Subject:** Supplier Labor-Risk Exposure and Governance Response  
**Course:** SSCM 201 — Corporate Social Responsibility, Ethics, and Stakeholder Governance

---

## Executive Summary

The organization faces material stakeholder risk due to allegations of labor abuse within a supplier-linked facility. Even if the facility sits outside the company’s direct payroll structure, the brand remains exposed because customers, regulators, and the public judge the full operating model, not only the legal boundary of direct employment.

Immediate action is recommended in supplier review, public communication, and governance oversight.

---

## Risk Description

The current risk is not only reputational.
It is operational, ethical, and governance-related.

The organization appears to have a gap between its public responsibility claims and its supplier oversight practices. That gap creates distrust and increases the likelihood of repeat failures.

---

## Affected Stakeholders

Affected stakeholders include:

- workers in the supplier network
- customers
- investors
- regulators
- employees
- leadership
- community partners

---

## Governance Weakness

The likely governance weakness is insufficient supplier monitoring combined with overreliance on formal vendor approval processes.

If subcontracting occurred outside the approved structure, the issue is not only supplier misconduct.
It is also weak control design.

---

## Recommended Immediate Actions

1. Launch an independent supplier investigation.
2. Freeze new sourcing through the affected channel until facts are established.
3. Review subcontracting controls across the supplier base.
4. Prepare a public statement grounded in accountability rather than denial.
5. Establish board-level reporting on supplier responsibility risk.

---

## Closing Assessment

The greatest danger is not only the original misconduct.
It is the appearance that the organization’s responsibility claims exceed its actual controls.

A governance-centered response is therefore necessary.
