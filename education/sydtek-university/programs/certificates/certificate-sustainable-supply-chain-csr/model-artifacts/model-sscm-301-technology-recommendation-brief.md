# Model SSCM 301 Artifact
## Technology Recommendation Brief

**Title:** Evaluating Traceability Options for a Mid-Sized Food Distributor  
**Course:** SSCM 301 — Technology for Supply Chain Transparency and Resilience

---

## Problem Definition

The organization wants stronger traceability for premium food products but currently relies on fragmented records across suppliers, internal spreadsheets, and shipping documentation.

The problem is not simply a lack of technology.
It is a lack of consistent, trustworthy data flow.

---

## Recommendation

The organization should not begin with blockchain as its first move.

It should first standardize supplier data fields, improve internal event capture, and define which traceability questions matter most. Only after that foundation is established should leadership evaluate whether blockchain creates additional value.

---

## Why This Recommendation Fits

This recommendation fits because it addresses the actual bottleneck.

A ledger does not fix poor source data.
If upstream inputs are inconsistent, a more advanced technical layer may only create the appearance of control.

---

## Governance Requirements

Any traceability system will still require:

- supplier accountability
- data validation rules
- audit practices
- exception handling
- executive ownership

Technology should support governance, not replace it.

---

## Final Judgment

The strongest recommendation is a phased traceability strategy rather than a prestige-driven technology deployment.

This improves realism, lowers implementation risk, and better aligns digital investment with operational need.
