# Instructor Guide
## Certificate in Sustainable Supply Chain Management & Corporate Social Responsibility

This guide helps instructors, mentors, and program leads deliver the certificate consistently across SydTek University and SydTek Scholars.

---

## 1. Teaching Goal

The goal of this certificate is not just to expose students to sustainability language.

The goal is to help students develop judgment.

Students should finish this program able to:

- understand how supply chains work
- identify real points of fragility
- reason through ethical and governance failures
- evaluate technology realistically
- recommend practical improvements
- communicate clearly in professional formats

---

## 2. Recommended Teaching Style

Faculty should teach this certificate with a balance of:

- clarity
- seriousness
- practical realism
- structured feedback
- skepticism toward empty jargon

Instructors should continually redirect students from vague phrases toward operational detail.

### Example Redirects
Instead of:
“Sustainability is important for the future.”

Push toward:
“What specifically should change in sourcing, logistics, packaging, labor oversight, or reporting?”

Instead of:
“Blockchain creates trust.”

Push toward:
“What exactly is being verified, and what still depends on real-world data integrity?”

---

## 3. Delivery Model

This certificate is built for flexible delivery.

### It can be taught as:
- fully asynchronous
- low-residency online
- guided cohort format
- dual-enrollment or scholars pathway
- portfolio-based independent study with faculty review

---

## 4. Weekly Teaching Rhythm

A recommended weekly rhythm is:

### Early Week
- assign lecture, reading, or case
- introduce one core concept
- provide one applied example

### Midweek
- discussion or short written reflection
- guided analysis of a scenario or case
- peer or faculty check-in if appropriate

### End of Week
- assignment milestone or mini-deliverable
- brief instructor feedback
- preparation for next topic

This rhythm works well in both university and scholars settings.

---

## 5. What to Emphasize in Every Course

Across the certificate, instructors should repeatedly reinforce five themes:

### A. Supply chains are systems.
Students should always think in terms of relationships, dependencies, and tradeoffs.

### B. Responsibility must be operational.
Ethics and CSR only matter if they show up in decisions, controls, and workflows.

### C. Technology is conditional.
Students must learn to ask whether a tool actually fits the problem.

### D. Resilience often requires investment.
Students should understand that the cheapest system may also be the weakest.

### E. Good writing reflects good thinking.
Clear communication is part of professional competence in this field.

---

## 6. Common Weaknesses to Correct

### Weakness 1 — Abstract language
Students may use phrases like sustainable, ethical, resilient, or transparent without saying what they mean.

### Weakness 2 — Tool worship
Students may assume AI, blockchain, or dashboards automatically solve problems.

### Weakness 3 — Moral simplification
Students may treat ethical problems as obvious without tracing accountability, incentives, and structure.

### Weakness 4 — Unrealistic proposals
Students may recommend total transformation without prioritization, sequencing, or constraints.

### Weakness 5 — Shallow evidence
Students may make claims without enough explanation, examples, or logic.

---

## 7. Feedback Priorities

When commenting on student work, prioritize:

1. clarity
2. realism
3. evidence
4. systems thinking
5. quality of recommendation
6. communication to the intended audience

---

## 8. Suggested Grading Posture

Strong work should show:

- precise understanding
- realistic recommendations
- balanced ethical and operational reasoning
- thoughtful engagement with tradeoffs
- clear professional writing

Avoid over-rewarding polished wording when the analysis itself is weak.

---

## 9. Scholars Adaptation Guidance

For SydTek Scholars, faculty may adapt:

- reading load
- memo length
- complexity of case materials
- amount of scaffolding
- presentation format

But instructors should preserve the seriousness of the ideas.

Do not reduce the program to slogans or motivational content.

---

## 10. Capstone Teaching Guidance

In the capstone, keep students focused on these questions:

- What is broken?
- Where is the risk concentrated?
- What matters most to fix first?
- What is realistic now?
- What should be measured?
- Who is accountable?

These questions keep the work grounded.

---

## 11. Recommended Instructor Additions Over Time

As the program matures, instructors may add:

- sample case videos
- annotated model assignments
- discussion banks
- quiz banks
- example feedback language
- sample capstone projects
- industry guest sessions
